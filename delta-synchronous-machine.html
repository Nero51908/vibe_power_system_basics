Power systems / worked example / 50 Hz

The delta-connected synchronous machine

A 500 kVA, 12-lead diesel alternator leaves the hire yard linked for 415 V star. The job wants a three-wire 240 V supply (motor loads only, no neutral), so the links in the terminal box are moved and the same three windings now sit in a triangle. Nothing inside the machine has changed; every number at its terminals has. Running at 400 kW, 0.85 lagging, this page follows one winding and one line: which current the copper feels, which one the cable feels, where the 30° comes from, and why, inside each winding, the EMF E leads the terminal voltage by a load angle δ. Step 1: the delta, alive

Three windings in a triangle, three wires out

Here is the machine as the fitter wired it: three windings end-to-end in a triangle, a line conductor leaving each corner. Run the film and watch the currents themselves: each arrow is the instantaneous current in that element, flipping with its sign. Two things hold at every frame. The voltage across winding ab is the line-to-line voltage v_ab: one measurement, two names. The current in winding ab is not the current in line a: at corner a two windings meet one wire, and the wire carries the superposition of their two currents (a difference, once the reference directions are fixed).

Step 2: two windings, one wire, the first 30°

One terminal, two windings

Terminal a is where winding ab ends and winding ca begins, and the line conductor carries the superposition of the two winding currents that share it. With I_ab counted as arriving at a and I_ca as leaving, I_a = I_ab − I_ca (the same sum written as I_ab + I_ac is one button away). On the left the arrows close that superposition as a triangle; on the right it happens height by height at the time cursor. Two equal arrows 120° apart combine into an arrow √3 as long, sitting 30° behind the first; that is where the line-to-winding √3 and the 30° both come from. Flip the sequence in the deck and the 30° changes side.

Step 3: the second 30°, and why they cancel

Two shifts, one angle

Step 2 gave the current its 30°. The voltage has one too. The star-equivalent voltage V_an (what a phase-to-neutral voltmeter would read if the machine had a neutral) is V_ab/√3 and sits 30° behind V_ab. Line current lags winding current by 30°; star voltage lags line voltage by 30°; so the angle from V_an to I_a is the same φ as the angle from V_ab to I_ab. Pair a line current with a line-to-line voltage instead and you read φ + 30°, the oscilloscope trap. And because every arrow turns at the same ω, all four keep their relative angles in every frame: that rigid rotation is the whole licence for reading a running machine off one photograph.

Step 4: inside one winding (E, V and the quarter turn)

Where δ lives

Inside winding ab the field produces an EMF E_ab; what reaches the terminals is V_ab. The difference is the drop across the synchronous reactance, and X_s is not a phasor. It has no wave. It is an instruction: take I_ab, turn it a quarter turn ahead, scale it by X_s. The result of that instruction is a phasor, j·X_s·I_ab, and it has a wave; lay it on the tip of V_ab and E_ab closes the triangle. The angle δ between E and V is the load angle, and on the right it is a time: e_ab(t) crosses zero before v_ab(t) by δ/360°·T. More torque moves δ, not the speed; both waves keep the same 20 ms.

Step 6: diagnostics

Four ways to misread the delta

Each misreading leaves a fingerprint. The cards recompute from the deck; if your number disagrees with the page by one of these exact patterns, you have found the mistake.

Line current in the winding equation

E = V_ab + j·X_s·I_a  (should be I_ab) –×√3 drop, twisted 30°

–. The winding sees its own current. Plugging the line current in makes the drop √3 too long and turns it 30° the wrong way: E comes out far too large and δ far too small.

The 30° trap

pf = cos ∠(V_ab, I_a) = cos(φ ± 30°) –30° too much (or too little) lag

–. A scope across a-b with a clamp on line a reads the angle between a line-to-line voltage and a line current. Element voltage against element current, always (Step 3).

Star equivalent with the full X_s

E_an = V_an + j·X_s·I_a  (should be X_s/3) –×3 reactance

–. Three windings of X_s in delta look like three of X_s/3 in star (Z_Y = Z_Δ/3). Carry the delta ohms into the star picture and the triangle is drawn three times too tall.

"A lagging generator absorbs vars"

Q = 3·V·(E·cos δ − V)/X_s –sign trap

In generator convention a lagging current means the machine is over-excited and supplies vars. "Absorbs" is what the same two words mean under motor convention. The var direction is physics; lag and lead are bookkeeping, which is why this page prints the direction beside every lag/lead label.
