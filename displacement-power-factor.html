Step 1 · Read the scope

An angle you can read as a time

cos φ = 0.85 sounds abstract until you put both waves on a scope. The current's zero-crossing arrives Δt = φ/360° × 20 ms after the voltage's — a lag you could measure with a stopwatch, if your stopwatch read microseconds. Flip the toggle in the deck and the current crosses early instead: leading. The phasor inset is the same fact frozen — I sits at −φ from V.

Step 2 · Instantaneous power

Multiply the waves

Point-by-point, p(t) = v(t)·i(t) splits into two machines: an always-forward hump P_1·(1−cos 2ωt) that averages to real power, and a zero-average slosh −Q_1·sin 2ωt that ships energy to the load and hauls it straight back, twice per cycle. The slosh is reactive power — it does nothing useful and it still has to fit down the wire.

Step 3 · Apparent power

The triangle you pay for

Average the hump and the slosh separately and you get the power triangle: P on the horizontal, Q on the vertical, and the hypotenuse S = √(P²+Q²) — the quantity the conductors actually feel, because the amperes follow S, not P. Capacitors at the bus supply vars locally, eating the vertical leg from the top down: slide Q_c and watch the hypotenuse — and the current — shrink.

Drawn to the S = V·I* convention (IEEE Std 1459 and every current textbook): the conjugate turns the current's −φ lag into +φ, so an inductive load has positive Q and its vars stand above the P axis even though the current itself lags. Push Q_c past Q and the triangle flips below the axis — the bus is now leading, and the hypotenuse starts growing again. Overcorrection is just undercorrection in a mirror.

Step 5 · Harmonics

Displacement earns its name

Now let the current distort — a classic six-pulse rectifier draws 5th and 7th harmonics on top of the fundamental. Slide THD_i up and watch what does not move: cos φ_1 stays exactly where the pf slider put it, because φ is an angle between fundamentals. What falls is the true power factor P/S = cos φ_1 × I_1/I_rms — the harmonic amperes add RMS without adding watts.

Harmonic mix: I_5 = √0.8·THD·I_1 and I_7 = √0.2·THD·I_1 (so their RMS sum is exactly THD·I_1), zero-phase on the fundamental. The fix is split down the middle: capacitors repair displacement, filters repair distortion — and a cos-φ meter and a true-pf meter on the same feeder disagree by exactly the distortion factor.
Step 6 · Diagnostics

Three ways to get vars wrong

Each mistake has a fingerprint. The cards recompute from the sliders — poke the deck and watch the wrongness track the truth.

The subtraction var

Q = S − P = – MVAr ? –real Q — √(S²−P²)

P, Q and S make a right triangle, not a shopping receipt. Vars live on the vertical leg, not in the arithmetic leftover — subtracting the legs of a triangle has never once produced its third side.

The optimistic meter

quote cos φ = – as "the pf" at 40% THD –true pf — ×0.93 kVA missed

A cos-φ meter reads the fundamentals and misses every harmonic ampere. At 40% THD the true pf is lower by exactly the distortion factor – — size the transformer off the optimistic number and it runs hot.

The overcorrector

bolt on Q_c = 250 MVAr → Q′ = – –current rising again

Past the P axis the triangle regrows on the other side: I_L is back up to – A against the – A minimum at exact correction. Vars cost money in either direction — set Q_c on the slider to 250 and watch Step 3 flip.
