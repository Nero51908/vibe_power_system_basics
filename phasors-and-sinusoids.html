Step 1 · One arrow, one wave

The projection machine

One arrow of length V_rms turns counterclockwise at ω. The instantaneous voltage is the projection of its √2-stretched tip — the point riding the dashed outer ring — onto the reference axis, drawn pointing up here so that height can be carried straight across; the arrow itself stays RMS length. The wave on the right is nothing but this rotation seen frame by frame; the ring, √2 larger than the arrow, is where the crest lives.

Freeze any frame — that photograph is the phasor diagram; spinning the photo is just changing the reference, and nothing physical changes. The dashed projection line carries the ring point (the true crest value) across to the wave cursor; the arrow itself stays RMS length.
Step 2 · The balanced trio

Three arrows, one machine

Same machine, three arrows welded 120° apart and turning as one rigid set. Three projections, three waves — and because the arrows are balanced, the three instantaneous values sum to exactly zero at every frame of the film. Watch the ledger below compute it live.

–
That standing zero is the flagship page's dashed neutral rail: what cancels here is the voltage sum, but feed a balanced load and the three currents inherit the same 120° geometry and cancel identically — so the return conductor would carry nothing at every frame, and we don't build it.
Step 3 · Lag, in degrees and in milliseconds

The angle that is also a time

A phase shift is not just an angle on a protractor — it is a time on the clock: a current lagging by φ crosses zero Δt = φ/360°·T later than its voltage. Step 4 · Coda

Why three-phase exists

Multiply the two waves of Step 3 and the per-phase power p_a(t) = v_a(t)·i_a(t) pulses at 2ω: average VI·cos φ, ripple ±VI. With any lag at all it even dips negative twice a turn — energy sloshing backward toward the source. Add the three phases and every pulsation cancels: the sum is dead flat.

Each phase shakes; the shaft does not. Single-phase traction was rough enough to need special railway supplies — everything industrial is three-phase for this one picture. At φ = 0 the per-phase range is 0…200.0 MW and the sum 300.0 MW.
Step 5 · Diagnostics

Three ways to misread the film

Each misreading leaves a fingerprint. The cards recompute from the sliders — if your number disagrees with a phasor calculation by one of these exact factors, you have found the mistake.

The √2 impostor

arrow |I| = – A rms · crest = √2·|I| = – A –×√2 fingerprint

Reading the phasor length as the peak. The arrow is RMS; the wave it generates crests √2 higher. If a scope peak and a phasor magnitude disagree by exactly 1.414, this is why.

Lag means later

Δt = φ/360°·T = – ms –sign trap

A LAGGING current peaks and crosses zero after the voltage, not before. Students who shift the wave the wrong way carry −φ into every power calculation that follows.

The frequency mixer

Phasors freeze e^(jωt) at one shared ω; arrows spinning at different speeds have no fixed relative angle, so their sum is not a phasor. Every diagram on this page silently assumes one frequency — fine print that matters again on the harmonics page.
